AI Lead Scoring System

A system that reads a sales lead's behaviour and answers one question reps actually care about: “Who should I call first?” It scores every lead 0–100, labels it Hot, Warm, or Cold, and recommends the next action — served through a live API and a simple dashboard.

01 · The problem

Sales teams waste most of their time on leads that never convert

A typical CRM hands a rep a flat list of hundreds of leads with no signal about which ones are worth a call. So effort gets spread evenly — the lead who requested a demo yesterday gets the same attention as one who went cold three months ago. Industry studies put the wasted effort at 60–70% of a rep's time.

Who it's for: B2B sales and revenue teams, and the RevOps people who feed leads into a CRM. The goal is simple — turn an undifferentiated list into a ranked, explained priority order.

02 · What it does

Every lead gets a score, a tier, and a recommended next step

Give it a lead's basic CRM fields (source, industry, company size, engagement signals) and it returns:

TierScoreWhat it meansRecommended action
Hot80–100High conversion signalCall immediately
Warm50–79Engaged, not readyInvite to a demo / nurture
Cold0–49Low signal for nowAutomated nurture / re-qualify

It works one lead at a time (/api/v1/score) for real-time use inside a form or CRM webhook, or in bulk (/api/v1/score/batch, up to 1,000 leads) for a nightly re-scoring job.

03 · Try the live demo

How to see it working in 30 seconds

  • Open the dashboard. A status bar at the top shows when the model is live (give it a minute if it's waking up).
  • On “Score a Lead”, fill in a lead — e.g. source Referral, Enterprise size, a few website visits, demo requested = yes — and submit. You'll get a score, a Hot/Warm/Cold tier, and the recommended action.
  • Try a weak lead (cold source, no demo, interaction 90+ days ago) and watch the score and recommendation change.
  • “Batch Score” lets you score many leads at once and see them ranked highest-first.

No sign-up, no real data — the inputs are harmless sample fields and nothing is stored.

The frontend is a React/Vite app on Vercel. The brain is a Python FastAPI service running in a Docker container on Render. When a request arrives, the service engineers features from the raw lead, asks the XGBoost model for a conversion probability, and turns that into the final score and recommendation.

How the 0–100 score is built (and why it's explainable)

The score isn't just the model's raw probability. It's a transparent blend so a human can reason about it:

  • 60% — the model's conversion probability (the learned signal)
  • 25% — an engagement score from behaviour (website visits, email clicks, demo requested)
  • 15% — a recency score that decays as time since the last interaction grows

There's also a behavioural floor: a highly engaged, very recent lead can't be buried by a low model probability alone. That keeps the ranking sensible to a salesperson, not just to the math.

ROC-AUC ≈ 0.89 on a held-out test set. In plain terms: given one lead that converted and one that didn't, the model ranks the converter higher about 89% of the time — well above the 50% you'd get from guessing. The data is imbalanced (~12% convert), so the model is trained with scale_pos_weight to stay sensitive to the rare positive class rather than just predicting “no”.

A logistic-regression baseline is included for honesty and interpretability — XGBoost is the production choice because it captures the non-linear interactions (e.g. demo requested × engagement) that a linear model misses.

Important: this ROC-AUC is measured on synthetic data whose patterns I designed. It validates the pipeline and method; real CRM data would need its own training and evaluation before any claim about production accuracy.

07 · Security & operations

Safe to leave running in public

  • Rate limiting: a per-IP limit on the scoring endpoints protects the free tier from accidental floods and abuse (the limiter fails open, so a bug never blocks real traffic).
  • Locked-down CORS: the API only accepts browser calls from the known dashboard origin, not *.
  • No cold-start surprise: a scheduled job pings the health endpoint every 10 minutes so the demo stays warm for visitors.
  • Hardened container: the app runs as a non-root user with a Docker HEALTHCHECK, and there are no secrets in the code or the repo.
  • Input validation: every field is validated by Pydantic — out-of-range or unknown values are rejected with a clear 422 instead of a crash.
08 · Known limitations

What this version is not

  • Synthetic data. The model learns patterns I designed, not real buyer behaviour. Real deployment needs real historical CRM data.
  • Free-tier hosting. One small instance, one region, and a cold start after idle periods. Fine for a demo, not for production SLAs.
  • Stateless scoring. Scores aren't persisted or tracked over time — there's no feedback loop retraining the model on outcomes yet.
  • No authentication on the public demo. It's open on purpose for evaluation, protected by rate limiting rather than login.

10 · Lessons learned

What I'd carry into the next project

  • A score people trust beats a score that's slightly more accurate. Blending the model probability with explainable engagement and recency signals made the ranking defensible to a non-technical user.
  • Imbalanced data needs deliberate handling. With a 12% positive rate, accuracy is a misleading metric — ROC-AUC and class weighting were the right tools.
  • “Deployed” is a feature, not an afterthought. Cold starts, CORS, and rate limiting are what separate a notebook from something you can actually hand a stranger a link to.
  • Be explicit about synthetic data. Stating the limitation up front builds more credibility than a big accuracy number without context.
